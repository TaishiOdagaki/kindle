> **At a glance**
> - *Evangelion* looks like a giant-robot-versus-monster story, but its setting rests on a few interlocking secrets: what the Angels are, what the Evas are, and what two organizations, SEELE and NERV, each want from them.
> - The series is generous with names and sparing with explanations. Much of what fans "know" about this world comes from official companion materials, creator interviews, and inference rather than from the episodes themselves.
> - This chapter sorts every claim into **Shown**, **Stated**, or **Interpreted**, and ends with a list of what the work never explains.
> - **Spoilers: full.** If you have not seen the series, read the Primer first.

---

## 10.1 How this chapter works

Three labels are used throughout this book (see *How to Read This Book*).

| Label | Meaning | Example of the kind of source |
|---|---|---|
| **Shown** | Established on screen, by image or dialogue | An episode, a film |
| **Stated** | Established by a creator's statement or an official document | An interview, an official guidebook |
| **Interpreted** | A reading offered by this book or by critics | Fan analysis, criticism |

Unless a passage says otherwise, "the series" means the 1995–96 television broadcast. The 1997 films and the *Rebuild* films change or add details; those differences are collected in Chapter 23. Where an element behaves differently across versions, a **Version note** says so.

A note on sources. Fan encyclopedias are useful for finding things, and this chapter does consult them as leads. But they sometimes disagree with one another, and they sometimes present inference as fact. Section 10.9 shows an example. Nothing in this chapter should be treated as settled until its `CHECK` mark has been cleared against the work itself.

## 10.2 The world before the story

The series begins in the year **2015** [[CHECK: confirm the on-screen/official setting year and where it is first given]]. The world it describes has already been broken once.

| Event | What the work says | Level |
|---|---|---|
| **Second Impact**, 13 September 2000 | The public account is that a meteorite struck Antarctica. [[CHECK: date and the "meteorite" cover story: where each is given]] | Shown |
| The real cause | Second Impact was triggered by a contact experiment on the first Angel, **Adam**, carried out by a research party known as the **Katsuragi Expedition**, with SEELE behind it. [[CHECK: where this is revealed and who says it]] | Shown |
| Aftermath | Rising seas, climate disruption, war, and the loss of a large part of the world's population. [[CHECK: the exact figure the series gives, if any]] | Shown |
| Construction of **Tokyo-3** | The fortified city built to host NERV's headquarters, begun in the years after Second Impact. [[CHECK: year construction begins]] | Shown / Stated |
| **Sachiel** attacks | The Third Angel's arrival opens the series in 2015. | Shown |

Two points matter for everything that follows.

First, **the public story and the true story differ**, and the series holds the true story back for a long time. A viewer who reads the world through episode 1's information will form a picture that later episodes correct. This is deliberate, and it is one reason first and second viewings feel so different.

Second, **Misato Katsuragi is tied to Second Impact personally**; she survived it [[CHECK: where this is shown or stated]]. The series uses the world's history to explain its characters before it explains its mechanics.

## 10.3 The Angels

### What the series calls them

In Japanese the enemies are *shito* (使徒), a word that means "apostle" or "disciple" in a religious sense. The English-language releases render it as **"Angel."** The difference matters for interpretation: "apostle" implies someone sent, whereas "angel" implies a messenger. Readers working only from English should keep in mind that "Angel" is a translator's choice. [[CHECK: confirm 使徒 as the on-screen term; note how official English subtitles render it]]

### Numbering

The Angels are numbered. **Adam** is the First Angel and **Lilith** the Second; the creatures NERV fights in the television series run from the **Third** to the **Seventeenth** [[CHECK: confirm Adam = First and Lilith = Second in an official source, and that the fought Angels are Third–Seventeenth]]. Their personal names (Sachiel, Shamshel, and so on) are drawn from Jewish angelology and appear in official materials and on NERV displays. ### What they share

- **They are alien in form and varied in shape.** There is no standard body plan. [[CHECK]]
- **Each has a core.** Most Angels are defeated by destroying a red sphere-like core. [[CHECK: confirm for each Angel, and note exceptions]]
- **They carry an AT Field**, the barrier discussed in section 10.5, which must be neutralized before they can be hurt. [[CHECK: first episode where this is explained]]
- **Their signature is detected by NERV's analysis.** NERV's computers classify them by a pattern readout. [[CHECK: the term shown on screen, often given as "Pattern Blue"]]
- **They are drawn to the depths beneath NERV headquarters.** NERV's leadership says the Angels are trying to reach the being held there. [[CHECK: who says this, and in which episode]]

### What they are

The work's answer, given piecemeal, is that the Angels are **offspring of Adam**, the First Angel, and that humanity descends from **Lilith** (the series' term for human beings is "Lilin"; Kaworu uses it in episode 24 [[CHECK]]). In other words, the Angels and humans are two lines of life, and the conflict between them is a conflict between siblings. This is a **Shown / Stated** reading, but the series never spells out *why* the Angels attack, *why* in this sequence, or *why* now. Those questions are collected in section 10.8.

## 10.4 Adam, Lilith, and the figure in the depths

Beneath NERV headquarters, in a region called the **Terminal Dogma**, a huge pale figure hangs crucified, pierced by a spear. [[CHECK: first on-screen appearance and how it is named at that point]] Viewers meeting this figure are first told it is **Adam**. The series later reveals it to be **Lilith**. [[CHECK: the exact episode(s) and wording of the reveal; confirm whether the TV series or The End of Evangelion carries it]]

This is one of the most important facts in the work, and also one of the easiest to misstate. It means that:

1. NERV's headquarters is built around Lilith, not Adam;
2. the "Adam" that Gendo Ikari holds is a **separate** object, an embryo-like form kept in his possession [[CHECK: where the embryo is shown and described]]; and
3. the Angels' target, which the staff describe as Adam, is a statement about what NERV *believes* at a given point in the story. [[CHECK: whether any character states the target is Adam, Lilith, or both]]

The **Lance of Longinus** is a spear associated with the crucified figure. [[CHECK: where it is shown, how it is named, and its role in the finale]] Its function in the plot is covered in sections 10.7 and 10.10.

## 10.5 The Evangelions

### What they are

The Evangelion units ("Evas") are enormous biomechanical combat machines. The title card of episode 1 labels them as a "general-purpose humanoid decisive weapon," an "artificial human." [[CHECK: exact on-screen text in episode 1]] They are not simple robots: they are **organic** at their core, and they bleed, regenerate, and move on their own. [[CHECK: first episode where an Eva acts without its pilot]]

The Eva's armor is described within the series as **restraint** rather than protection. [[CHECK: who says this and in which episode]]

### Units and pilots

| Unit | Pilot | Introduced |
|---|---|---|
| Unit-00 (prototype) | Rei Ayanami | [[CHECK]] |
| Unit-01 (test type) | Shinji Ikari | Episode 1 |
| Unit-02 | Asuka Langley Soryu | [[CHECK]] |
| Unit-03 | Toji Suzuhara | [[CHECK]] |
| Mass Production Evas | (unmanned) | The End of Evangelion [[CHECK]] |

The pilots are called **Children**: Rei is the First Child, Asuka the Second, Shinji the Third, Toji the Fourth, Kaworu Nagisa the Fifth. [[CHECK: confirm the order and each introduction. Confirm the "Marduk Institute" as the selection body and where it is named.]] The age of the pilots is a central element of the story: the key characters are fourteen. [[CHECK: Stated or Shown for each character]]

### How they are piloted

- The pilot sits in an **entry plug** inserted into the Eva. [[CHECK]]
- The plug is filled with **LCL**, an oxygenated liquid the pilot breathes. [[CHECK]]
- The pilot's mind is connected to the Eva's. The measure of how well it works is the **synchronization ratio**; low ratio means poor control, and in some circumstances a pilot cannot move the Eva at all. [[CHECK: first on-screen use]]
- If synchronization is extreme, the pilot's boundary with the Eva can blur. [[CHECK: the relevant scene, and which episode shows it]]

### Power

Evas run on external power through an **umbilical cable**; cut it and they fall back on an **internal battery** for a very limited time. [[CHECK: the battery duration given in the series. Secondary sources say five minutes; confirm]] Some Angels and Evas have an **S² organ** ("super-solenoid") that gives them an internal source of energy. [[CHECK: exact name, how it is explained, which units have it]]

The dramatic image of the Eva running out of power is a staple of the series' action scenes. It also gives the action a clock.

### Berserk

At least twice, Unit-01 acts without its pilot's control, defeating an Angel with ferocity the staff cannot explain. [[CHECK: the two episodes. Secondary sources point to 4 and 19.]] The series treats this as a sign that the Eva is more than a machine, and it is directly connected to the question of what the Eva's soul is.

### The soul in the machine

The Evas contain **souls**, in a sense the series never fully defines. Unit-01 holds the soul of **Yui Ikari**, Shinji's mother. [[CHECK: where this is revealed and how]] This is **Shown / Stated**, but its implications are **Interpreted**: it recasts every scene of Shinji piloting as a scene between a son and a mother.

### The AT Field

The **AT Field** is a barrier that all Angels and Evas can produce. "AT" is explained as "Absolute Terror." [[CHECK: first explanation and the source of the expansion]] Within the story, it has two meanings that the series deliberately lets overlap:

- **Physical:** it is a defensive wall that must be neutralized before damage can be done. [[CHECK]]
- **Psychological:** it is described as the wall of the self, the boundary that separates one mind from another. Kaworu describes it this way in episode 24, and it is also the central idea behind Instrumentality. [[CHECK: exact wording in episode 24, and in episodes 25–26]]

This overlap is the work's most important metaphor, and Chapter 26 builds on it. The point to hold here is the factual one: **the same word covers a weapon and an emotional boundary, and the series does not separate them.**

## 10.6 The organizations

### NERV

NERV is a special agency operating under the United Nations. Its headquarters sit beneath Tokyo-3, in a vast underground space called the **Geofront**. [[CHECK: the UN relationship and the "Geofront" term]]

| Role | Person |
|---|---|
| Commander | Gendo Ikari |
| Vice Commander | Kozo Fuyutsuki |
| Operations director | Misato Katsuragi |
| Chief scientist | Ritsuko Akagi |

[[CHECK: titles as given on screen and in official materials]]

NERV's official mission is to protect humanity from the Angels. Its commander's own mission, as the later episodes show, is different (section 10.7).

### Gehirn

**Gehirn** is NERV's predecessor organization, responsible for building the first Evangelion and the facilities under Tokyo-3. [[CHECK: where Gehirn is named and what it is said to have done]] ("Gehirn" is the German word for "brain.") Gendo Ikari led its project, and Naoko Akagi, Ritsuko's mother, was its chief scientist. [[CHECK]]

### The MAGI

The **MAGI** are a trio of supercomputers that run NERV's systems. They are named **Melchior, Balthasar, and Casper**. The series reveals that they were built around the personality of Naoko Akagi, each reflecting a different aspect of her: scientist, mother, woman. [[CHECK: names as shown on screen; where the personality design is revealed; which episode]]

### SEELE

**SEELE** (German for "soul") is a secret council that stands above NERV. It is made of twelve members, who appear as black monoliths bearing numbers, and its leader is **Keel Lorenz**. [[CHECK: the count; whether "twelve" is stated on screen or in official materials]] It funds and directs NERV, and it holds a body of texts called the **Dead Sea Scrolls**, which it treats as a script for coming events. [[CHECK: how the series presents the Scrolls and who says what about their contents]]

## 10.7 Two plans for one world

The most important structural fact about the world is that **NERV's commander and SEELE do not want the same thing**, though for most of the series they appear to be allies.

### SEELE's scenario

SEELE's plan, as the series shows it, is to bring about **Third Impact** and **Human Instrumentality**: the dissolution of the boundaries between human beings so that humanity becomes a single entity. [[CHECK: where SEELE states this; wording]] They rely on the Scrolls' prophecy and on the **Mass Production Evas**. [[CHECK]]

### Gendo's plan

Gendo Ikari's goal, as revealed late, is a different version of the same event: using Lilith and Adam to reunite with **Yui**. [[CHECK: where it is revealed; confirm the exact content as shown]] This is a **Shown** fact in outline, but the details are **Interpreted**: the series gives Gendo's motive in fragments, and critics disagree on how coherent it is (see the debate *Gendo's motive*).

### What is Instrumentality?

In the television series, the **Human Instrumentality Project** is presented as a process that gathers human souls into a single whole. [[CHECK: the TV series' own definition]] In Episodes 25 and 26, it is depicted largely as an inner event, a series of psychological scenes in which Shinji reconsiders himself. In *The End of Evangelion*, the same event is depicted as a physical catastrophe. The relationship between these two is the subject of Chapter 15.

### Third Impact

**Third Impact** is the event that would trigger Instrumentality. [[CHECK: where the term is defined]] "First Impact" is mentioned only in passing in the series, and its nature is not explained. [[CHECK: whether and where "First Impact" is mentioned]]

## 10.8 What the work never explains

A reader who wants "the complete explanation" should know that the work itself leaves several large gaps. These are **not** missing from this book; they are missing from the work.

1. **Why the Angels attack in sequence**, and what determines the order.
2. **What the Angels want**, beyond reaching the being in the Terminal Dogma.
3. **The nature of Lilith**, and how humanity descends from it.
4. **The content of the Dead Sea Scrolls**, beyond fragments that characters quote.
5. **Why only children can pilot the Evas**, beyond the statement that they are selected. [[CHECK: what the series says about the selection]]
6. **The role of the Black Moon and the White Moon**, which are mentioned and never fully used. [[CHECK: whether and where these are named]]
7. **The relationship between the Evas and Adam or Lilith**: sources disagree (see 10.9).

Chapter 30 treats the major open questions as **debates**, with the positions that critics and fans have taken.

## 10.9 Where the sources disagree

A caution about secondary sources, drawn from the research for this chapter itself. One fan encyclopedia summary describes Unit-01 as a "direct clone of Lilith." Others describe the Evas as copies of Adam. [[CHECK: what the work shows or states about the origin of each Eva unit; this is a genuine open point]] Neither summary is wrong because the writers are careless; the work's own presentation changes between the series and the films, and different sources emphasize different scenes.

The lesson for readers is practical. When a fact about *Evangelion*'s world seems oddly specific and oddly certain, look for the scene or the creator's statement behind it. If there is none, it is probably **Interpreted**.
