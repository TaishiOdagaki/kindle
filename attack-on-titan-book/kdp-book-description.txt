<p><b>Did Attack on Titan earn its own ending — or betray the story it spent twelve years building?</b></p>

<p>What began as a horror manga about giants eating people behind a wall became one of the most divisive, most-discussed endings in modern manga history — and one of the few shonen-adjacent stories ever accused of, and defended against, real political nationalism. This is a chapter-by-chapter critical companion built to take both sides of that argument seriously, written by a Japanese researcher for fans reading the series from outside Japan.</p>

<h4>What's inside:</h4>
<ul>
<li>The full history underneath the fiction: the Warrior program, the walls as a map of class, and why one plot beat reads as a structural echo of Pearl Harbor</li>
<li>The nationalism controversy laid out precisely — what's confirmed, what's still disputed, and why the sourcing matters</li>
<li>How this story landed differently in Japan, South Korea, China, Hong Kong, Israel, Palestine, and the United States — including a real Congressional censure the story's imagery got pulled into</li>
<li>A full-spoiler chapter on exactly what happens at the end, and a separate chapter making the actual case for why the fandom split in half over it</li>
<li>The studio switch nobody wanted, the theme song that outsold the show, and the numbers behind a franchise that became, for one year, the single most in-demand television show on Earth</li>
<li>My own verdict — argued directly, not hedged into a survey of everyone else's opinion</li>
</ul>

<p>Every factual claim in this book was checked against its source before it was written down. Where the record is genuinely disputed — including the murkiest, most reputationally sensitive controversy this book covers — that uncertainty is preserved on the page rather than smoothed into false certainty.</p>
