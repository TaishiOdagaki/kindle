# KDP / Amazon Ads Strategy for This Project's Books

This skill exists to accumulate real, book-specific advertising knowledge over time, the same way `verify-nonfiction-claims` grew out of this project's own factual mistakes rather than generic writing advice. It starts thin, on purpose. Every time a real campaign decision gets made here and its outcome becomes visible, add a dated entry to "What we've learned" below — that log is the actual point of this file, more than the checklist above it.

## Reading a performance snapshot without overreacting

- **Small-account KDP ad spend produces tiny sample sizes.** A few thousand yen of spend and single-digit purchase counts are not enough data to call a campaign "working" or "broken." Before changing strategy based on one week's numbers, check the cumulative window (30 days is usually the first meaningful one) and don't treat a single zero-sales week as a verdict — it's very likely noise at this scale.
- **Separate the CTR problem from the conversion problem.** Impressions → clicks tells you whether the ad is being shown to and noticed by a plausible audience. Clicks → purchases tells you whether the product page itself closes the sale once someone's there. A reasonable CTR (roughly 0.3%–1%+ is normal for Sponsored Products) with zero downstream purchases points at the listing (cover thumbnail, title, price, description) or at mismatched targeting — not at ad visibility. Don't "fix" the wrong half of the funnel.
- **The single highest-leverage, lowest-effort action available on any dashboard check-in**: open the "underperforming targets" / search-term report, sort by clicks descending with zero orders, and pause or negative-match the ones that are clearly off-target. Worth doing on essentially every review, regardless of anything else that's happening.
- **The KDP listing-keyword field (7 slots, on the book's own detail page) and Amazon Ads manual keyword targeting are different tools — never reuse one list for both.** KDP's field feeds a search-indexing algorithm that tokenizes and recombines the phrase; it doesn't need to be something a shopper literally types, which is why character-name clusters like "Eren Yeager Mikasa Levi Armin" work fine there. An Ads keyword only earns impressions when it matches a real typed query — that same phrase would sit dead in an ad campaign because nobody searches it verbatim. When setting up manual ad targeting, write natural search-query phrases from scratch (title-based: "attack on titan book"; intent-based: "attack on titan ending explained"; genre-based: "manga analysis book") — don't just copy the KDP field. See "What we've learned" below for the actual corrected ad-keyword list used for Attack on Titan.
- **Cross-check active ad targeting against the actual purpose of the book's KDP keyword list, not the literal phrases** (see each book's own `README.md` → KDP keyword section for what's being positioned around). If a campaign is running on Amazon's automatic targeting, pull the search-term report and confirm the queries actually driving clicks resemble genuine high-intent search behavior — not generic franchise-name traffic, and not KDP-metadata-style phrases that were never meant to be typed verbatim in the first place.
- **Budget too small to learn from is a real, distinct failure mode from "the campaign doesn't work."** If total weekly clicks are in the single digits, the targeting algorithm doesn't have enough signal to optimize. Before concluding a keyword set or targeting strategy has failed, make sure the campaign actually got enough traffic (rough rule of thumb: tens of clicks, not single digits, per week) to judge it fairly.
- **A book's own README keyword list and its live ad targeting can drift apart** — the keyword list gets updated in this repo, but nothing pushes that update into an already-running Amazon Ads campaign automatically. Treat a mismatch between the two as a routine thing to check, not a one-time setup step.
- **KDP's own listing-keyword field (the 7-slot "Keywords" field on the book's own detail page, distinct from Amazon Ads targeting) has real compliance rules, and KDP enforces them by email review, not just at submission time.** Never put a real living creator's name (even the actual subject of the book, in a biography/interview framing) or another separate work's own trademarked title in that field — both are explicit, demonstrated rejection triggers for this project. See "What we've learned" below for the incident this rule came from.

## What we've learned (dated log — add to this, don't just read it)

- **2026-09-20 — first campaign snapshot reviewed.** ¥1,096 spent over ~30 days, 3 purchases, ¥1,365 sales, ROAS 1.25 (net positive, but on an n of 3 — not enough to trust yet). Most recent 7-day window: 706 impressions, 8 clicks, 0 conversions. CTR (~1.1%) was fine; the stall was purely at the conversion step. Sample size judged too small to draw a real conclusion either way. Recommended next steps: (1) open the "clicks but zero sales" target list and prune obviously off-target entries, (2) confirm auto-targeting search terms actually match the book's KDP keyword positioning, (3) let the campaign accumulate more clicks before revisiting rather than reacting to one flat week. No budget or bid changes were made at this checkpoint. Outcome of the pruning step: not yet known — update this entry (or add a new one) once it is.
- **2026-09-20 — two KDP compliance rejections in one day, both traced to reusable rules.** (1) KDP rejected the "Criticism & Literary Studies > Comics & Graphic Novels" category for *Chainsaw Man* and *Demon Slayer*, on the grounds that a category containing "Comics" misleads readers when the book is prose criticism, not a comic — even though that category is structurally meant for criticism *about* comics. The user considered disputing this by email (the category selection was arguably correct on the taxonomy's own logic) rather than just switching to a safer category, judging from past experience that KDP support replies quickly; a dispute reply was drafted. Outcome of the dispute: not yet known. (2) Separately, KDP rejected *Demon Slayer*'s KDP-listing keyword field for including "Koyoharu Gotouge" (the real manga creator) and "Infinity Castle" (a different movie's trademarked title) — this one was not disputed, just fixed directly, since it clearly matched KDP's own stated "other authors' names" / "unauthorized brands" prohibitions. All three books' keyword lists were corrected the same day, including live edits to Chainsaw Man's already-published listing (confirmed applied by the user). Lesson: when a rejection is genuinely defensible on the platform's own logic, disputing may be worth the time cost given fast past response times; when a rejection clearly matches a stated rule, fix it immediately rather than testing the boundary.

- **2026-09-21 — Demon Slayer went live; drafted Sponsored Brands custom text ad copy.** The book was successfully published (the category/keyword fixes from the day before did not block publication). Asked for Sponsored Brands "custom text" ad copy — first assumption (unverified web search) was a 50-character headline limit; the user corrected this to the actual field they were looking at, which allows 150 characters. Lesson: confirm the actual character limit shown in the live Ads Console field before drafting, rather than trusting a generic web search result — Amazon has multiple Sponsored Brands ad formats with different limits (short headline vs. longer custom-text formats), and guessing wrong wastes a drafting round. Also applied the "no other work's trademarked title" rule from the keyword-rejection incident to ad copy itself (dropped an early draft using "Infinity Castle," a separate movie's title, as unsafe by the same logic even though ad copy is a different KDP/Ads surface than the listing-keyword field). Final chosen copy, 149 characters, after two rounds of tone revision (more emotional, then "fan" swapped for "scholar" for a more authoritative register): "One Japanese scholar's years of study, written for every reader still hoping Tanjiro finds his happy ending — the history, the heartbreak, the truth." Outcome (CTR/conversion on this specific ad copy): not yet known — update this entry once it's run for a while.

- **2026-09-21 — computed the real breakeven ROAS; it's higher than it looks.** All three books are priced at $2.99 with the 70% royalty tier. Royalty per sale ≈ $2.99 × 0.70 minus a small delivery fee (negligible for these text-only files) ≈ ~$2.01. Breakeven ROAS = list price ÷ royalty per sale = $2.99 ÷ $2.01 ≈ **1.5**, not 1.0 — a ROAS reading above 1.0 still means the campaign is losing money once KDP's cut is accounted for. Checked against the actual campaign history: ROAS was 1.25 on 2026-09-20 and had fallen to 1.01 by 2026-09-21 (same 3 purchases/¥1,365 sales both times, cost crept up ¥258 with no new conversions) — meaning this campaign has likely been net-unprofitable at every point measured so far, even when it looked "positive" by the naive sales-over-cost reading. Recommendation given: hold off launching a third (Attack on Titan) campaign until the existing two are pruned and ROAS is retested against the ~1.5 line, rather than spreading the same underlying conversion problem across a third book. Whether that recommendation was followed, and what ROAS the campaigns show after pruning: not yet known.

- **2026-09-21 — an "empty" search term report doesn't mean "zero sales," it means "zero clicks."** Amazon's own template description confirms the Search Term report only lists terms with at least 1 click — a genuinely empty CSV (header row only) means no clicks at all occurred in the queried window, not that clicks happened without converting. Worth stating explicitly because it's easy to misread "no rows" as "no sales" when actually it's a stronger, different signal (no engagement at all). This came up twice: first for a mismatched-campaign-filter false alarm (see the 09-20 entry about the unrelated 日本語教員試験 product sharing this Ads account), then genuinely for both the Chainsaw Man and Demon Slayer campaigns' search-term reports on this date, which turned out to be brand-new campaigns (started the day before) rather than a reporting error. New Sponsored Products campaigns typically take 24–72 hours after creation before Amazon actually starts serving impressions (ad review delay), so a same-day or next-day empty report is expected, not a red flag — recheck in 2–3 days rather than treating it as a failure signal.
- **This Amazon Ads account also runs an unrelated product line (登録日本語教員試験 exam workbooks) alongside these manga companion books.** Any screenshot, CSV, or dashboard view pulled from this account needs its campaign/product confirmed before being analyzed here — a search-term report for the exam workbook was mistakenly brought into this skill's analysis once already before being caught. See `nihongo-kyoin-ads-strategy` for that product line's own log; keep the two separate.
- **2026-09-21 — caught a real design mistake before launch: KDP listing keywords ≠ Ads targeting keywords.** When setting up Attack on Titan's first ad campaign, the plan was to reuse the book's 7 KDP listing-keyword-field entries directly as manual ad targeting keywords. The user caught this before it launched: phrases like "Eren Yeager Mikasa Levi Armin" are valid KDP metadata (Amazon tokenizes/recombines it for search indexing) but would get zero impressions as a literal ad keyword, since no shopper types that exact string into the search bar. Corrected ad-specific keyword list, written as natural search queries instead: "attack on titan book," "attack on titan analysis," "shingeki no kyojin book," "attack on titan ending explained," "aot companion book," "manga analysis book," "anime companion book." The KDP field itself was left unchanged — it's serving its own, different purpose correctly. Lesson generalized into the checklist above; apply the same natural-query rewrite before launching Chainsaw Man's or Demon Slayer's next ad keyword refresh too, since both of those campaigns may have inherited the same mistake if their manual targeting (if any) was ever set from the KDP list directly.

- **2026-09-21 — launched Attack on Titan's first Sponsored Products campaign, manual keyword targeting, US marketplace ($USD).** Set up with the 7 corrected natural-query keywords (see the entry above), phrase match only (broad and exact were unchecked — a "推奨" default suggestion the UI had auto-added, unrelated to the chosen list, was removed before finalizing). Bid: Amazon's Ads console had a recommended bid for only 1 of the 7 keywords; the other 6 got a system-suggested fallback bid of $0.75, which was accepted as-is rather than guessed at manually, since going lower risked the keyword winning zero auctions entirely. Also confirmed a UI/terminology point worth remembering: **Sponsored Products campaigns have no "custom text" field** — the ad creative is generated automatically from the existing product listing (title, cover, price, reviews). Custom text (the 150-character headline used for Demon Slayer) only exists in **Sponsored Brands**, a separate campaign type that has to be created on its own, after or alongside the Sponsored Products one, not as a field within it. Chosen Sponsored Brands custom text for Attack on Titan (to be used once that separate campaign is created), 149 characters: "One Japanese scholar's honest verdict on whether Attack on Titan earned its ending — the real history, the controversy, and an argument worth having." Daily budget set at **$3/day** (raised deliberately above this project's earlier ¥100–200/day-equivalent norm) — reasoning: at a $0.75 bid, a $1-ish daily budget would cap out after 1–2 clicks and reproduce the same too-small-a-sample problem seen with the earlier campaigns; $3/day allows roughly 4–6 clicks/day, aiming for ~30–40 clicks over the one-week review window this campaign was set to, closer to the "tens of clicks" threshold this skill treats as the minimum for a fair read. Campaign duration: no hard end date required by the platform, but set to align with this project's now-standard one-week check-in cadence, matching Chainsaw Man's and Demon Slayer's campaigns. Outcome of this campaign: not yet known.
- **2026-09-21 — attempted a combined 3-book Sponsored Brands "product collection" campaign, hit a real eligibility gate, deferred it.** Author-targeted Sponsored Brands requires: (1) the pen name filed in Author Central, (2) an active Amazon Ads account in good standing, (3) a valid payment method — and separately, each individual book has to be explicitly added under Author Central's own "本" (Books) tab (search by title/ISBN/pen name → "この本を追加"), which is a different step from just registering the pen name itself. Amazon's own support panel states rights-holder confirmation for a newly added book/pen name can take up to 48 hours to propagate to the Ads account. The "キャンペーンを作成する" button stayed disabled even after the user registered the pen name in Author Central — likely either the per-book "add this book" step hadn't been done for all three titles, or the 48-hour verification window hadn't elapsed yet; not conclusively diagnosed. Decision: **deferred Sponsored Brands entirely** rather than troubleshoot further right now — the user's own reasoning was that a multi-book "product collection" ad is more worth running once the catalog has grown past three titles, not a technical blocker they needed solved immediately. Drafted copy is preserved for whenever this gets revisited: combined 3-book headline (150 chars) — "One Japanese scholar, three of anime's biggest stories — Chainsaw Man, Demon Slayer, and Attack on Titan, each explained with the same rigor and care." — plus a 7-keyword series-level list (not tied to any single title): "anime manga companion books," "manga criticism essay series," "otaku pop culture nonfiction," "japanese researcher anime books," "shonen manga analysis series," "anime ending explained books," "manga fan essay collection."

- **2026-09-21 — major correction: the "declining ROAS" data from earlier today was misattributed, not actually Chainsaw Man/Demon Slayer's.** Reviewing the full campaign list (6 campaigns total in this account) revealed Chainsaw Man's campaign started 2026-09-19 and Demon Slayer's started 2026-09-20 — both far too new to be the source of the ¥1,096→¥1,354, 3-purchases/¥1,365-sales, ROAS-1.25-declining-to-1.01 figures analyzed earlier. Those exact figures (3 purchases, ¥1,365 sales) turned out to match the now-ended "パーフェクト2026" (discontinued Japanese-teacher-exam edition) campaign precisely. Conclusion: the earlier dashboard views were **all-campaigns account-level aggregates**, not scoped to any single book, and the "existing campaigns are running below breakeven" conclusion was retracted — Chainsaw Man and Demon Slayer actually had zero real performance history at that point, not a proven loss. Lesson for future review: always confirm a dashboard/report is scoped to one specific campaign (check the campaign list, not just a performance chart) before attributing its numbers to one book — an account-level aggregate silently blends unrelated products' results. This account also runs a third unrelated campaign, "宝条四季、二十五歳" (paused), confirming the account holds at least three separate product lines' worth of campaigns.
- **2026-09-21 — switched Chainsaw Man and Demon Slayer from auto-targeting to manual, matching Attack on Titan.** Both had been running on オートターゲティング since their 9/19 and 9/20 starts; given the demonstrated auto-targeting drift risk (the discontinued exam-book campaign's "substitutes"/"close match" auto-buckets pulling in unrelated traffic), both were rebuilt as manual/phrase-match Sponsored Products campaigns. Natural-query keyword lists drafted the same way as Attack on Titan's: Chainsaw Man — "chainsaw man book," "chainsaw man analysis," "chainsaw man companion book," "chainsaw man ending explained," "chainsaw man manga guide," "manga analysis book," "anime companion book," plus "japanese culture" added after reviewing Amazon's own keyword-recommendation panel (it had real suggested-bid data, meaning genuine search volume, and matches this book's own "Japanese researcher" positioning). Demon Slayer — "demon slayer book," "demon slayer analysis," "kimetsu no yaiba book," "demon slayer companion book," "demon slayer manga ending," "manga analysis book," "anime companion book." Two of Amazon's own recommended keywords for Chainsaw Man were explicitly rejected: **"devil man"** — this is the title of a real, separate, unrelated manga/anime franchise (Go Nagai's *Devilman*), the same "another work's trademarked title" risk category as "Infinity Castle" before, even though this is ad targeting rather than the KDP listing-keyword field; and **"book 1"** — too generic (competes with every volume-1 product on Amazon) despite a comparatively high $1.18 suggested bid. "japanese book" and "devil fan" were also reviewed and set aside as lower-priority/ambiguous rather than included outright.
