# KDP / Amazon Ads Strategy for This Project's Books

This skill exists to accumulate real, book-specific advertising knowledge over time, the same way `verify-nonfiction-claims` grew out of this project's own factual mistakes rather than generic writing advice. It starts thin, on purpose. Every time a real campaign decision gets made here and its outcome becomes visible, add a dated entry to "What we've learned" below — that log is the actual point of this file, more than the checklist above it.

## Reading a performance snapshot without overreacting

- **Small-account KDP ad spend produces tiny sample sizes.** A few thousand yen of spend and single-digit purchase counts are not enough data to call a campaign "working" or "broken." Before changing strategy based on one week's numbers, check the cumulative window (30 days is usually the first meaningful one) and don't treat a single zero-sales week as a verdict — it's very likely noise at this scale.
- **Separate the CTR problem from the conversion problem.** Impressions → clicks tells you whether the ad is being shown to and noticed by a plausible audience. Clicks → purchases tells you whether the product page itself closes the sale once someone's there. A reasonable CTR (roughly 0.3%–1%+ is normal for Sponsored Products) with zero downstream purchases points at the listing (cover thumbnail, title, price, description) or at mismatched targeting — not at ad visibility. Don't "fix" the wrong half of the funnel.
- **The single highest-leverage, lowest-effort action available on any dashboard check-in**: open the "underperforming targets" / search-term report, sort by clicks descending with zero orders, and pause or negative-match the ones that are clearly off-target. Worth doing on essentially every review, regardless of anything else that's happening.
- **The KDP listing-keyword field (7 slots, on the book's own detail page) and Amazon Ads manual keyword targeting are different tools — never reuse one list for both.** KDP's field feeds a search-indexing algorithm that tokenizes and recombines the phrase; it doesn't need to be something a shopper literally types, which is why character-name clusters like "Eren Yeager Mikasa Levi Armin" work fine there. An Ads keyword only earns impressions when it matches a real typed query — that same phrase would sit dead in an ad campaign because nobody searches it verbatim. When setting up manual ad targeting, write natural search-query phrases from scratch (title-based: "attack on titan book"; intent-based: "attack on titan ending explained"; genre-based: "manga analysis book") — don't just copy the KDP field. See "What we've learned" below for the actual corrected ad-keyword list used for Attack on Titan.
- **Cross-check active ad targeting against the actual purpose of the book's KDP keyword list, not the literal phrases** (see each book's own `README.md` → KDP keyword section for what's being positioned around). If a campaign is running on Amazon's automatic targeting, pull the search-term report and confirm the queries actually driving clicks resemble genuine high-intent search behavior — not generic franchise-name traffic, and not KDP-metadata-style phrases that were never meant to be typed verbatim in the first place.
- **Budget too small to learn from is a real, distinct failure mode from "the campaign doesn't work."** If total weekly clicks are in the single digits, the targeting algorithm doesn't have enough signal to optimize. Before concluding a keyword set or targeting strategy has failed, make sure the campaign actually got enough traffic (rough rule of thumb: tens of clicks, not single digits, per week) to judge it fairly.
- **A book's own README keyword list and its live ad targeting can drift apart** — the keyword list gets updated in this repo, but nothing pushes that update into an already-running Amazon Ads campaign automatically. Treat a mismatch between the two as a routine thing to check, not a one-time setup step.
- **KDP's own listing-keyword field (the 7-slot "Keywords" field on the book's own detail page, distinct from Amazon Ads targeting) has real compliance rules, and KDP enforces them by email review, not just at submission time.** Never put a real living creator's name (even the actual subject of the book, in a biography/interview framing) or another separate work's own trademarked title in that field — both are explicit, demonstrated rejection triggers for this project. See "What we've learned" below for the incident this rule came from.
- **A keyword with zero impressions ("no bid data" in the targeting tab) is a fundamentally different problem than a keyword with real impressions but no conversions yet — don't treat them the same.** The former has essentially no addressable search volume for that exact phrase and isn't costing anything either, so there's no urgency to prune it (removing a $0-spend keyword doesn't free up budget for anything else). The latter just hasn't accumulated enough clicks — this account has twice now seen a bare/generic keyword go 0-for-several-clicks before converting once real volume built up (see `nihongo-kyoin-ads-strategy`'s 2026-09-25 entry for the concrete case: "日本語教員試験" was flagged as a negative-match candidate twice, then converted at ROAS 1.51 on its 13th click). Don't negative-match or delete a keyword just because early clicks didn't convert — check whether it's actually getting impressions first.
- **The KDP sales dashboard and an Amazon Ads "all campaigns" view are both account-wide aggregates, same as the campaign-list dashboard that caused the 2026-09-21 ROAS misattribution.** Neither is scoped to one book by default. A "this month's royalty minus this month's ad spend" comparison pulled from these two top-level screens is a legitimate account-wide profit/loss snapshot, but it blends every title in the account (all Jiro Naozane books plus the unrelated 登録日本語教員試験 line) — never present it as one book's P&L without decomposing by title first (KDP's per-title order report, and each campaign's own individual performance tab).

(2) Separately, KDP rejected *Demon Slayer*'s KDP-listing keyword field for including "Koyoharu Gotouge" (the real manga creator) and "Infinity Castle" (a different movie's trademarked title) — this one was not disputed, just fixed directly, since it clearly matched KDP's own stated "other authors' names" / "unauthorized brands" prohibitions. Lesson: when a rejection is genuinely defensible on the platform's own logic, disputing may be worth the time cost given fast past response times; when a rejection clearly matches a stated rule, fix it immediately rather than testing the boundary.

- **2026-09-21 — Demon Slayer went live; drafted Sponsored Brands custom text ad copy.** The book was successfully published (the category/keyword fixes from the day before did not block publication). Also applied the "no other work's trademarked title" rule from the keyword-rejection incident to ad copy itself (dropped an early draft using "Infinity Castle," a separate movie's title, as unsafe by the same logic even though ad copy is a different KDP/Ads surface than the listing-keyword field). Outcome (CTR/conversion on this specific ad copy): not yet known — update this entry once it's run for a while.

- **2026-09-21 — computed the real breakeven ROAS; it's higher than it looks.** All three books are priced at $2.99 with the 70% royalty tier. Royalty per sale ≈ $2.99 × 0.70 minus a small delivery fee (negligible for these text-only files) ≈ ~$2.01. Breakeven ROAS = list price ÷ royalty per sale = $2.99 ÷ $2.01 ≈ **1.5**, not 1.0 — a ROAS reading above 1.0 still means the campaign is losing money once KDP's cut is accounted for. Checked against the actual campaign history: ROAS was 1.25 on 2026-09-20 and had fallen to 1.01 by 2026-09-21 (same 3 purchases/¥1,365 sales both times, cost crept up ¥258 with no new conversions) — meaning this campaign has likely been net-unprofitable at every point measured so far, even when it looked "positive" by the naive sales-over-cost reading. Recommendation given: hold off launching a third (Attack on Titan) campaign until the existing two are pruned and ROAS is retested against the ~1.5 line, rather than spreading the same underlying conversion problem across a third book. Whether that recommendation was followed, and what ROAS the campaigns show after pruning: not yet known.

- **2026-09-21 — an "empty" search term report doesn't mean "zero sales," it means "zero clicks."** Amazon's own template description confirms the Search Term report only lists terms with at least 1 click — a genuinely empty CSV (header row only) means no clicks at all occurred in the queried window, not that clicks happened without converting. Worth stating explicitly because it's easy to misread "no rows" as "no sales" when actually it's a stronger, different signal (no engagement at all). This came up twice: first for a mismatched-campaign-filter false alarm (see the 09-20 entry about the unrelated 日本語教員試験 product sharing this Ads account), then genuinely for both the Chainsaw Man and Demon Slayer campaigns' search-term reports on this date, which turned out to be brand-new campaigns (started the day before) rather than a reporting error. New Sponsored Products campaigns typically take 24–72 hours after creation before Amazon actually starts serving impressions (ad review delay), so a same-day or next-day empty report is expected, not a red flag — recheck in 2–3 days rather than treating it as a failure signal.
- **This Amazon Ads account also runs an unrelated product line (登録日本語教員試験 exam workbooks) alongside these manga companion books.** Any screenshot, CSV, or dashboard view pulled from this account needs its campaign/product confirmed before being analyzed here — a search-term report for the exam workbook was mistakenly brought into this skill's analysis once already before being caught. See `nihongo-kyoin-ads-strategy` for that product line's own log; keep the two separate.
- **2026-09-21 — caught a real design mistake before launch: KDP listing keywords ≠ Ads targeting keywords.** When setting up Attack on Titan's first ad campaign, the plan was to reuse the book's 7 KDP listing-keyword-field entries directly as manual ad targeting keywords. The user caught this before it launched: phrases like "Eren Yeager Mikasa Levi Armin" are valid KDP metadata (Amazon tokenizes/recombines it for search indexing) but would get zero impressions as a literal ad keyword, since no shopper types that exact string into the search bar. Corrected ad-specific keyword list, written as natural search queries instead: "attack on titan book," "attack on titan analysis," "shingeki no kyojin book," "attack on titan ending explained," "aot companion book," "manga analysis book," "anime companion book." The KDP field itself was left unchanged — it's serving its own, different purpose correctly. Lesson generalized into the checklist above; apply the same natural-query rewrite before launching Chainsaw Man's or Demon Slayer's next ad keyword refresh too, since both of those campaigns may have inherited the same mistake if their manual targeting (if any) was ever set from the KDP list directly.

- **2026-09-21 — launched Attack on Titan's first Sponsored Products campaign, manual keyword targeting, US marketplace ($USD).** Set up with the 7 corrected natural-query keywords (see the entry above), phrase match only (broad and exact were unchecked — a "推奨" default suggestion the UI had auto-added, unrelated to the chosen list, was removed before finalizing). Bid: Amazon's Ads console had a recommended bid for only 1 of the 7 keywords; the other 6 got a system-suggested fallback bid of $0.75, which was accepted as-is rather than guessed at manually, since going lower risked the keyword winning zero auctions entirely. Also confirmed a UI/terminology point worth remembering: **Sponsored Products campaigns have no "custom text" field** — the ad creative is generated automatically from the existing product listing (title, cover, price, reviews). Custom text (the 150-character headline used for Demon Slayer) only exists in **Sponsored Brands**, a separate campaign type that has to be created on its own, after or alongside the Sponsored Products one, not as a field within it. Campaign duration: no hard end date required by the platform, but set to align with this project's now-standard one-week check-in cadence, matching Chainsaw Man's and Demon Slayer's campaigns. Outcome of this campaign: not yet known.
