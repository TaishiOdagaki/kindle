# Tyler Atsumori: author persona

## Established facts (all supplied or approved by the user)

- Pen name Tyler Atsumori; imprint Japanese Culture Press; pronoun "he."
- Born and raised in Kyoto to an American mother and a Japanese father.
- Studied modern and contemporary Japanese literature at a university in the Kansai region.
- Research focus: how Japanese novelists, Murakami especially, are read and argued about in Japan versus how they are received in translation. Reads the Japanese-language criticism and prize-jury remarks that English-language writing tends to skip.
- **Signature human detail (user's wording, 2026-10-02):** every morning during his run he listens, again and again, to Murakami's works on audiobook (Audible) to absorb the rhythm and signature of Murakami's Japanese. Sometimes he concentrates on listening more than on running and has put a brand-new, much-loved pair of Asics running shoes straight into a puddle.

For Vol.2 and Vol.3 keep the two running-and-puddle sentences; change only the sentence about sources (Vol.2: historical scholarship on the events Murakami's fiction engages with; Vol.3: recent criticism and the debate over the Nanjing passage).

It is not Murakami's version, which comes with triathlon training. It is a jog. Every morning he sweats through the same question: what does making sentences have to do with holding your own pace and getting to the end without walking?"

So each volume gets its own running story, consistent with the same runner: Vol.1 audiobooks-and-Asics; Vol.2 twenty years of jogging and the question about pace and not walking. Vol.3: ask the user for a third story rather than inventing one.

Running thread across the series: Vol.1 audiobooks and the Asics in a puddle; Vol.2 twenty years of jogging and the question about pace and not walking; Vol.3 Kyoto summer heat and the mantra. Keep the same runner and do not contradict earlier volumes.

## Rules for reuse

- Keep the running motif consistent across volumes, but each volume may carry its own story (see Vol.2 above). Do not invent new anecdotes about the author; ask the user.
- Keep the bio to about three sentences. Front matter spends the free sample.
- **End the bio on its best, funniest line** (the puddle), not on credentials or a "sources" sentence.
- Do not claim credentials the user has not given (no degree names, employers, awards).

## How to write a human author profile (pattern for any pen name)

- A specific, slightly odd, behavioral detail beats an adjective. "Deeply passionate about Japanese literature" reads as AI; "puts new shoes in a puddle because he is listening instead of looking" reads as a person.
- The detail should connect to the book's subject (here, Murakami's own running essay is the source of the opening scene).
- Ask the user for the detail; do not manufacture it. Record it here before reusing it.
- Sibling persona for the anime books (Jiro Naozane): "still buys the physical volumes after reading the digital release first, still gets pulled into arguments about an ending months after everyone else has moved on" (see `kdp-conversion-design` on the festive-clarke-cnoct4 branch).
